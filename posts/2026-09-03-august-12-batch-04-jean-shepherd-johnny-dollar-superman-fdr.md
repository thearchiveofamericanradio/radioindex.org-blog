### Key Historical & Archival Insights Extracted

1. **Jean Shepherd's Raw Requiem for Lenny Bruce (August 12, 1966)**: Recorded live late on a humid Friday night over WOR New York just days after Lenny Bruce was found dead in the Hollywood Hills, Jean Shepherd departed completely from his usual midwestern boyhood nostalgia. Shepherd delivered a landmark, deeply moving 45-minute meditation on Bruce's artistry, the crushing weight of institutional prosecution, and how Bruce's jazz-inflected stream-of-consciousness fundamentally altered American satire.
2. **Superman and the Golden Age of Transcribed Serials (August 12, 1940)**: Beginning the celebrated "Mayan Treasure" story arc, Bud Collyer effortlessly transitions between the mild-mannered cadence of Clark Kent and the booming baritone of Superman. Backed by Kellogg's Pep, this storyline exemplifies the crisp sound design and rapid cliffhanger pacing that made the Mutual serial an afternoon staple for millions of Depression-era youths.
3. **FDR's Puget Sound Strategic Address (August 12, 1944)**: Stepping ashore at the Puget Sound Navy Yard in Bremerton after a secretive 7,000-mile Pacific inspection tour, President Franklin D. Roosevelt spoke directly from the deck of a warship. He briefed the nation on his historic Honolulu summit with General Douglas MacArthur and Admiral Chester Nimitz, outlining the decisive final phase of the island-hopping campaign against imperial Japan.
4. **The Twilight of Radio Drama: Johnny Dollar in August 1962**: Broadcast only seven weeks before the final sign-off of *Yours Truly, Johnny Dollar* and *Suspense* (which ended network radio drama on September 30, 1962), "The Oldest Gag Matter" stars Mandel Kramer in fine, cynical form. Produced in CBS's storied Studio 1 in New York under Bruno Zirato Jr., it demonstrates the razor-sharp acting that persisted right up to the medium's curtain call.
