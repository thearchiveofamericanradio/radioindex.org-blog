## August 12 Forensic Archival Sweep (Batch 3)

Advancing across **August 12**, Batch 3 enriches 25 historical broadcasts spanning 1927 through 1975 across national networks, wartime shortwave relays, and syndicated transcriptions. In strict accordance with our zero-synthesis mandate, every program title, broadcast timeslot, commercial sponsor, production credit, and narrative synopsis has been verified against ground-truth audio transcriptions, contemporary newspaper radio directories, and station logs.

This batch is anchored by remarkable historical milestones: Captain Clark Gable's wartime 8th Air Force combat report broadcast from London with NBC's Don Hollenbeck; an extraordinary 1927 live remote from Thomas A. Edison's Glenmont estate celebrating the Golden Jubilee of the phonograph; Spike Jones and His City Slickers turning *The Chase and Sanborn Program* into musical mayhem in Santa Monica; William Conrad and Norman Macdonnell's hard-hitting *Gunsmoke* installment "Snakebite"; and Edward R. Murrow and Elmer Davis reporting live on the climactic Battle of Britain air duels over the English Channel.

### Key Archival & Production Highlights

1. **Capt. Clark Gable's 8th Air Force Dispatch from London (August 12, 1943)**: Enlisting in the Army Air Forces following the tragic death of Carole Lombard, Hollywood superstar Clark Gable was commissioned as an aerial gunner and combat photographer with the 351st Bombardment Group based at RAF Polebrook. In this historic live shortwave report to NBC, correspondent Don Hollenbeck interviews Captain Gable, who speaks modestly and earnestly about the courage of American B-17 Flying Fortress aircrews enduring intense flak and fighter attacks over occupied Europe while filming the documentary *Combat America*.
2. **Thomas Edison's 50th Phonograph Jubilee at Glenmont (August 12, 1927)**: Broadcast live over NBC's fledgling network from the lawn of Edison's Victorian estate in West Orange, New Jersey, this transmission preserves an 80-year-old Thomas Alva Edison commemorating the Golden Jubilee of recorded sound (1877–1927). Joined by New Jersey Governor A. Harry Moore, Edison famously addressed the radio microphone—referring to the medium with playful rivalry as a "mere upstart" compared to his phonograph—and recited his historic inaugural line: *"Mary had a little lamb."*
3. **Spike Jones in Santa Monica on Chase and Sanborn (August 12, 1945)**: Serving as the high-spirited summer replacement for Edgar Bergen and Charlie McCarthy on NBC, Spike Jones and His City Slickers broadcast live from the Santa Monica municipal ballroom. With sweet-voiced GI pinup Frances Langford providing the glamorous counterpoint, Jones unleashes his trademark synchronized pistol shots, washboards, klaxons, and slide whistles in blistering send-ups of popular ballads.
4. **Gunsmoke: "Snakebite" (August 12, 1956)**: Written by John Meston and produced/directed by Norman Macdonnell, Episode #225 of radio's definitive adult Western illustrates the gritty emotional realism that defined the series. William Conrad's Matt Dillon confronts the stark psychological despair of an isolated frontier homesteader whose dying act of revenge challenges the moral compass of Dodge City.
