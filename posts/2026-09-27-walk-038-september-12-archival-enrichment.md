**Date**: 2026-09-27  
**Author**: radio index  
**Tags**: National Defense Test Day, John J. Pershing, John W. Weeks, John J. Carty, Charles M. Saltzman, Cast and Crew, Radio Logs

The National Defense Test Day recording already held by The Archive of American Radio preserves speeches, telephone roll calls, and General John J. Pershing's farewells to four corps commanders. Reviewing the full surviving recording supplies eight named speaker credits and a separate technical crew credit for John J. Carty.

Pershing speaks with Bullard in New York, Hale in Chicago, Duncan in Omaha, and Morton in San Francisco. The [National Archives' Third Annual Report, 1936–1937](https://www.archives.gov/files/about/history/sources/reports/1937-annual-report.pdf) identifies their recorded voices alongside Pershing and Saltzman. Carty's Director credit describes the telephone and broadcast arrangements acknowledged by Pershing at about 31 minutes. Pershing's portrait is selected as the primary credit image.

The opening announcer, individual telephone operators, and a later speaker whose name is corrupted in the automatic captions remain unidentified. The notes retain those limits; names mentioned in the audience do not establish speaking credits.

## The newspaper schedule

The *Birmingham Age-Herald* explicitly prints **9:15 p.m. E.S.T.** for the program and names WCAP, WEAF, KSD, and WFAA. That time is preserved as printed. [September 12, 1924, page 2](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85038485/1924-09-12/ed-1/?sp=2).

The *Evening Star* lists the Washington program at **9:15 o'clock** and identifies Saltzman, Carty, Weeks, and Pershing as speakers. Its coverage also names WOAW, WFAA, and KGO. [September 12, 1924, page 36](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1924-09-12/ed-1/?sp=36).

These citations come from the radio-log lookup's newspaper page crops. The Birmingham listing supplies the explicit time zone; no local listing has been converted to Eastern time.

## What survives

Western Electric recorded the event onto discs. Two sides were lost during processing, according to the [Library of Congress recording essay](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/NationalDefenseTest.pdf). The reviewed transfer therefore preserves the surviving recording, with that loss documented in its notes.

This is one special event. The missing disc sides do not establish numbered installments of a serial, so they do not create speculative story parts in the catalog.

The War Department receives the program's sponsor credit. The full surviving recording contains no commercial sponsor spot. WCAP, WEAF, and KGO have separate broadcaster records.
