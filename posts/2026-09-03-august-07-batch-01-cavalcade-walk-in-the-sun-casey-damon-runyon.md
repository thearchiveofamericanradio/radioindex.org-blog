### Key Historical & Archival Insights Extracted

1. **Wartime Realism on Cavalcade**: DuPont's broadcast of "A Walk in the Sun" aired while Allied forces were pushing through Normandy and Italy in the late summer of 1944. Rather than standard heroic propaganda, Harry Brown's dialogue captured the honest fears, gallows humor, and exhaustion of infantry riflemen.
2. **Anchor Hocking's Milestone**: The August 7, 1947 broadcast of *Casey, Crime Photographer* marked Anchor Hocking Glass Corporation's 1st full anniversary as sole sponsor, highlighted in the spoken dialogue by announcer Bob Hite alongside commercial spots for Fire-King heat-resistant glass ovenware.
3. **Runyon's Broadway Vernacular**: *The Damon Runyon Theater* captured the unmistakable Runyonesque dialect—referring to women as "dolls", money as "the scratch", and streetwise gamblers with names like Silk and Sorrowful—brought alive by John Brown's deadpan delivery.
