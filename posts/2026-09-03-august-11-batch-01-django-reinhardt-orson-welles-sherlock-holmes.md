### Key Historical & Archival Insights Extracted

1. **Django Reinhardt Live from Paris (August 11, 1949)**: Broadcast directly from Club Saint-Germain in post-liberation Paris for *This Is Paris*—the first regular international series originated from Europe for American listeners—Maurice Chevalier introduces the incomparable Django Reinhardt. Armed with his signature Selmer acoustic guitar, Reinhardt performs scorching gypsy jazz improvisations that remain among the rarest surviving live radio captures of his post-war period.
2. **Orson Welles' Double Tour de Force (August 11, 1946)**: August 11, 1946 represents an extraordinary double pinnacle for Orson Welles. On Sunday afternoon for Lear Radio on ABC, Welles delivered one of American radio's most famous civil rights manifestos: responding directly to the blinding of Black WWII veteran Isaac Woodard by a South Carolina police chief, Welles proclaimed that *"to be born free is to be born in debt"* and excoriated white supremacist reactionaries. Hours later on CBS, his *Mercury Summer Theatre of the Air* presented Commander Edward Ellsberg's polar disaster epic *Hell on Ice*, dramatizing the crushed hull and frozen endurance of the 1879 USS Jeannette expedition, accompanied by Bernard Herrmann's haunting score.
3. **Carleton Hobbs as the Canonical BBC Sherlock Holmes (August 11, 1959)**: Michael Hardwick's dramatization of Arthur Conan Doyle's *The Adventure of the Copper Beeches* features the quintessential BBC Light Programme partnership of Carleton Hobbs and Norman Shelley. The episode meticulously documents the Gothic unease of Violet Hunter's mysterious employment at the Hampshire estate, faithfully reproducing Doyle's forensic dialogue.
4. **Eyewitness History: 'You Are There' in the English Channel (August 11, 1947)**: Under Robert Lewis Shayon's direction, *You Are There* (originally *CBS Is There*) transformed radio drama into immediate broadcast journalism. In this landmark early installment, Harry Marble and roving battlefield reporters describe Sir Francis Drake's fire ship assault on Philip II's Armada on July 29, 1588 with the staccato intensity of WWII network wire correspondents.
