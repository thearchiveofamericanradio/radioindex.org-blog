## August 11 Forensic Archival Sweep (Batch 3)

Advancing across **August 11**, Batch 3 enriches 25 historical broadcasts spanning 1939 through 1979 across network, syndication, and international airwaves. In accordance with our zero-synthesis standard, every recording was transcribed via acoustic speech recognition (`faster-whisper`), paired with WebVTT subtitle manifests, and cross-referenced against contemporaneous radio logs, newspaper schedules, and sponsor ledgers.

Highlights include Stan Kenton's live NBC remote from the Crescent Gardens Ballroom at Revere Beach; Chuck Schaden's backstage oral history interview with Sid Caesar at the Drury Lane Theatre; Gordon MacRae and Dorothy Warenskjold starring as Victorian poets in *The Railroad Hour*; Angela Lansbury's tense performance in *The MGM Theater of the Air*; Les Paul and Mary Ford demonstrating the revolutionary "Les Paulverizer"; Carleton Hobbs and Norman Shelley in the BBC's *The Copper Beeches*; and twin eras of *The Lone Ranger* (1939 with Earle Graser and 1955 with Brace Beemer).

### Key Archival & Production Highlights

1. **Stan Kenton Live from Revere Beach (August 11, 1953)**: Carried coast-to-coast over NBC via WBZ Boston, this live "Concert in Miniature" remote captures Stan Kenton's progressive jazz orchestra at the historic Crescent Gardens Ballroom on Massachusetts' North Shore. Kenton spotlights electric guitar virtuoso Sal Salvador on Bill Holman's adventurous composition "Frivolous Sal", underpinned by Stan Levey's driving drum work.
2. **Sid Caesar's Drury Lane Reminiscence (August 11, 1971)**: Recorded backstage at Chicago's suburban Drury Lane Theatre while Caesar starred in Neil Simon's *Plaza Suite*, broadcaster Chuck Schaden captures Caesar in prime storytelling form. Caesar reflects on his Broadway breakout in *Make Mine Manhattan* (1948) alongside David Burns, his machine-mimicry sound effects on *Your Show of Shows*, and his classic philosophical anthropomorphism of a white-wall automobile tire.
3. **The Railroad Hour's Literary Musical Drama (August 11, 1952)**: Lawrence & Lee (Jerome Lawrence and Robert E. Lee, later of *Inherit the Wind* fame) penned this original biographical musical *The Brownings* for Gordon MacRae and lyric soprano Dorothy Warenskjold. Conducted by Carmen Dragon with choral direction by Norman Luboff, the production translates Robert Browning's courtship of invalid poet Elizabeth Barrett into sparkling operetta.
4. **Les Paul's Multi-Track Wizardry (August 11, 1950)**: On their short-form NBC program, Les Paul and Mary Ford demonstrate the "Les Paulverizer"—the custom tape-delay controller mounted directly to Les's electric guitar. In between comic domestic spats about finding a regular day job, Les layers acoustic percussion taps, multitracked guitar harmonies, and rhythm riffs into an astonishing real-time rendition of Glenn Miller's "In the Mood".
5. **Angela Lansbury in Espionage Thriller *Stamboul Quest* (August 11, 1950)**: Hosted by MGM Vice President Howard Dietz on Mutual, this atmospheric radio adaptation of Sam Wood's 1934 film casts 24-year-old Angela Lansbury as master agent Annemarie (Fräulein Doktor), caught between national espionage imperatives and romantic devotion in World War I Turkey.
6. **The Lone Ranger: Evolution Across Sixteen Years (1939 vs. 1955)**: This batch captures the striking contrast between Earle Graser's hard-bitten pre-war Lone Ranger on Mutual/WXYZ (tracking ambushes east of Crow Ridge in "Abby's Murder Plan") and Brace Beemer's polished mid-1950s ABC incarnation (defending isolated rail trackwalkers in "Railroad to Prison", accompanied by General Mills' classic "Wheaties Blackboard" mail-away campaign).
