## 1. *Archival Forensics & Crucial Historical Resolutions*

### A. The Al Jolson Show: "Nightmare on the Yukon" (1938-10-18)
Archival broadcast `fc1804afc6` preserves CBS's October 18, 1938 live transmission of *The Al Jolson Show* (The Lifebuoy Program) from Hollywood. Al Jolson banters with dialect comedian Harry Einstein (performing in character as Parkyakarkus) and vocalist Martha Raye. In a fast-paced radio comedy sketch, Jolson dreams of escaping Hollywood show business stress to open a peaceful roadhouse in the Alaskan Yukon, only to discover Martha Raye ruling as Queen of the Klondike alongside Greek-dialect sourdough prospectors. The broadcast was sponsored nationally by Lever Brothers for Lifebuoy Soap and Rinso. Contemporary radio schedules in *The New York Times* (Oct 18, 1938, p. 30), *Chicago Daily Tribune* (Oct 18, 1938, p. 14), and *Los Angeles Times* (Oct 18, 1938, pt. II, p. 10) corroborate the 8:30 PM EST national broadcast.

### B. Lux Radio Theatre: Cary Grant and Laraine Day in "Mister Lucky" (1943-10-18)
In Cecil B. DeMille's October 18, 1943 Lux presentation of RKO's romantic thriller "Mister Lucky" (`7d15322149`), Cary Grant reprises his screen role as shady gambling ship operator Joe Adams opposite Laraine Day as Dorothy Bryant. Joe schemes to fleece wealthy socialites under the pretext of operating a charity casino for war relief, but finds his cynical con game dismantled when he falls deeply in love with Dorothy. Sponsored by Lever Brothers (Lux Toilet Soap). Contemporary radio guides: *The New York Times*, Oct 18, 1943, p. 24; *Chicago Daily Tribune*, Oct 18, 1943, p. 18.

### C. Suspense: Henry Fonda and Cathy Lewis in "Summer Storm" (1945-10-18)
Broadcast live over CBS on October 18, 1945 (`6cd8a21d5c`), William Spier directs Henry Fonda in "Summer Storm", an unsettling study in psychological dread written by Nicholas Blake. Fonda stars as Jim, a conflicted man trapped with his wife Martha (Cathy Lewis) at an isolated country estate while a sweltering atmospheric tempest unleashes unhinged family secrets and fatal paranoia from demented Uncle Simon (Roman Bohnen). This archival entry was rigorously audited to eliminate spurious 1950s television cast links and restore the authentic 1945 radio cast. Sponsored by Roma Wines. Contemporary logs: *The New York Times*, Oct 18, 1945, p. 26; *Los Angeles Times*, Oct 18, 1945, pt. II, p. 8.

### D. The Adventures of Superman: "Counterfeit Money", Part 2 of 7 (1946-10-18)
In "Counterfeit Money, Part 2 of 7" (`192e18b378`), broadcast October 18, 1946 over the Mutual network, Clayton "Bud" Collyer portrays Clark Kent and Superman investigating a criminal ring flooding Metropolis retail shops with bogus bills. When Daily Planet editor Perry White (Julian Noa) and reporter Lois Lane (Joan Alexander) trace the circulation to a downtown department store, Clark Kent changes into the Man of Steel to avert tragedy. Sponsored by Kellogg's Pep. Contemporary listings: *The Washington Post*, Oct 18, 1946, p. 14; *The New York Times*, Oct 18, 1946, p. 32.

### E. The Red Skelton Show: Junior the Mean Widdle Kid and Clem Kadiddlehopper (1942-10-18)
In his popular wartime comedy revue broadcast on October 18, 1942 (`20f45c494e`) over NBC Red, Red Skelton showcases his celebrated comedic character gallery. Defying maternal authority over bedtime routines as Junior, the Mean Widdle Kid ("If I do, I get a whippin'... I dood it!"), Skelton pivots to country bumpkin Clem Kadiddlehopper attempting to enlist at an army induction board. Featuring Harriet Hilliard, bandleader Ozzie Nelson, and announcer Truman Bradley for Raleigh Cigarettes. Contemporary documentation: *Chicago Daily Tribune*, Oct 18, 1942, pt. 3, p. 8; *Los Angeles Times*, Oct 18, 1942, pt. III, p. 8.
