## August 11 Forensic Archival Sweep (Batch 2)

Advancing our systematic calendar sweep across **August 11**, Batch 2 enriches 25 critical historical broadcasts spanning four decades of American and international broadcasting (1937–1965). Every program title, network timeslot, commercial sponsor, production credit, and narrative synopsis has been verified against ground-truth audio transcriptions (`faster-whisper`), contemporary newspaper radio logs, network program directories, and corporate ledgers.

This batch captures key moments in world history and radio culture: Radio Tokyo's shortwave propaganda in the climactic hours between the atomic bombings and Japan's surrender; Alex Dreier's NBC news analysis of Allied deliberations over Emperor Hirohito; Orson Welles's impassioned national radio address against white supremacy and southern poll taxes; Angela Lansbury starring in an MGM espionage thriller; and classic comedy, drama, and big band swing from Abbott & Costello, William Conrad's *Gunsmoke*, and Louis Prima.

### Cultural & Archival Findings

1. **August 11, 1945 — The Surrender Limbo**: Two broadcasts in this batch capture the world hanging by a thread on Saturday, August 11, 1945. On Radio Tokyo, Iva Toguri ('Orphan Ann') broadcast *The Zero Hour* to American soldiers in the Pacific, playing hot jazz while Japanese leadership debated capitulation terms. Concurrently on NBC in the United States, Alex Dreier delivered a searing news analysis sponsored by Red Heart Dog Food, explaining how the atomic bomb blasts at Hiroshima and Nagasaki and Soviet troops pouring into Manchuria shattered Imperial Japan, while Truman, Attlee, Stalin, and Chiang Kai-shek debated whether Emperor Hirohito would be permitted to keep his throne.
2. **Orson Welles's 'Moral Indebtedness'**: On August 11, 1946, Orson Welles took to ABC microphones following his relentless radio investigation into the blinding of decorated Black Army Sergeant Isaac Woodard by a South Carolina police chief. Welles delivered one of the most philosophical, unapologetic civil rights broadsides in network radio history, taking aim at southern poll taxes, segregation, and white supremacy, stating plainly: *"To be born free is to be born in debt. To live in freedom without fighting slavery is to profiteer."*
3. **Young Angela Lansbury in Espionage Noir**: Long before *The Manchurian Candidate* or *Murder, She Wrote*, a 24-year-old Angela Lansbury starred in Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer's transcribed *Theater of the Air* production of *Stamboul Quest* (August 11, 1950), hosted by MGM Vice President Howard Dietz. Lansbury gave a tour-de-force vocal performance as Fraulein Doktor, a master spy wrestling with conscience in post-war Istanbul.
4. **Authentic Commercial Textures**: From General Mills' cereal pitch for Kix ('that delicious new ready-to-eat breakfast material in golden bubbles') on Garry Moore's *Beat the Band*, to Sweetheart Soap's 1-cent sale on *We Love and Learn*, to Kroger's trademarked 'Tender-Ray Beef' cutting method on *Hearts in Harmony*, commercial spiels reflect the precision and cadence of wartime and postwar consumer marketing.
